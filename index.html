OUR MISSION

Welcome, Future Seahawk!

Starting college is exciting, but it can also feel confusing. For first-generation students, college may involve learning an entirely new set of expectations, vocabulary, and routines.

The hidden curriculum refers to the unwritten rules and expectations that students are often expected to understand without being taught. These can include how to approach professors, when to ask for help, how advising works, and where to find campus support.

First-generation students bring valuable strengths, perspectives, and experiences to campus. This guide is designed to make information easier to access, not to suggest that first-generation students lack ability.

THE HIDDEN CURRICULUM

Things Nobody Tells You About College

Click each topic to learn what it means and how to handle it.

What are professor office hours?

Office hours are designated times when professors are available to meet with students. You can ask questions about assignments, discuss course material, or request advice.

You do not have to be failing a class to attend office hours!

How do I email a professor?

Use a clear subject line, greet your professor respectfully, explain your question, and thank them for their time.

Example:

Subject: Question About Biology Assignment

Dear Professor Smith,
I hope you are doing well. I had a question about the upcoming assignment. Would you be able to clarify the expectations for the final section? Thank you for your time.

What is academic advising?

Academic advising helps students plan courses, understand degree requirements, and make decisions about their academic progress.

Meet with your advisor before registration and whenever you are uncertain about your academic plan.

What is a syllabus?

A syllabus is the guide to your course. It usually explains assignments, grading, attendance policies, learning goals, and important dates.

Read it early and check it throughout the semester.

What are credit hours?

Credit hours measure the academic credit associated with a course. They affect degree progress and sometimes enrollment status and financial aid eligibility.

What is FAFSA?

FAFSA stands for Free Application for Federal Student Aid. It is used to determine eligibility for various forms of financial assistance.

Check current deadlines and requirements through official financial aid resources.

Is asking for help a bad thing?

Absolutely not! Asking for help is an important college skill. Professors, advisors, tutors, and student support offices are there to assist you.

STUDENT VOICES

Advice From Real Students

Our project shares student perspectives on the college experience, including first-generation students from different universities.

FIRST-GENERATION STUDENT AT UNCG

Jason Scott

What is it like being the first to attend college in your family?

Jason shared that attending college was strongly encouraged by his family. His family worked hard to support their household and wanted him to have greater opportunities. Because of this, continuing his education after high school was an expectation. He explained that questioning whether to attend college would lead to a serious conversation with his family.

What do you wish you knew going into college?

Jason wishes he had known more about scholarships specifically available to first-generation college students. These opportunities could have helped cover his college expenses, but he discovered many of them after application deadlines had passed. He also wishes he had understood how flexible college schedules could be. After spending much of high school balancing football and academics, learning to create and manage his own schedule was a significant adjustment.

FIRST-GENERATION STUDENT AT ECU

Madison Carter

What was the most challenging part of transitioning from high school to college?

“The biggest challenge I faced was realizing how independent I had to be. In high school, teachers constantly reminded us about assignments and deadlines. In college, I had to learn how to manage my own time and stay organized without someone reminding me.”

What campus resource has been the most helpful?

“Academic advising has been really helpful because my advisor explained which classes I needed and helped me understand how to plan for future semesters, also my advisor has helped me figure out which teacher would best fit me.”

ADVICE TO FUTURE STUDENTS

5 Things We Wish We Knew

01

Ask Questions Early

You do not need to know everything before arriving at college. Asking questions is part of learning.

02

Get to Know Your Professors

Introduce yourself, participate in class, and attend office hours.

03

Use Campus Resources

Tutoring, advising, and library support are valuable even when your classes are going well.

04

Stay Organized

Keep track of assignments, registration dates, deadlines, and important emails.

05

You Belong Here

Being the first in your family to attend college is an achievement. Your experiences and perspective are valuable.
